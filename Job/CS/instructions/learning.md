# CS Learning, Review, and Testing

Explain intuition, a precise definition, assumptions, a worked example, and relevant tradeoffs. Adapt depth to the learner; use Chinese by default with English technical terms unless requested otherwise. For algorithms, discuss correctness and complexity; for systems, discuss mechanisms, failure modes, and constraints. Distinguish established results from assumptions and uncertain claims. Verify version-dependent details with primary documentation when needed.

At the start of teaching, review, or testing, check the skills actually available in the current runtime. If a relevant tutor or subject-specific skill is available, use it to guide explanation, active recall, and transfer practice without requiring the learner to invoke it. Ground the lesson in the selected book or existing CS materials. Skills supplement the source and workflow here; do not let them replace source attribution, assessment boundaries, or CS progress records. If none is available, use this workflow normally.

For `知识点复习`, follow [book-review.md](book-review.md): explain and discuss first; begin assessment only after the learner explicitly requests it. The following exercise rules apply when exercises or testing are requested.

Use small exercises to check understanding. During review or testing, ask one question at a time, let the learner attempt it, and avoid revealing the solution first. Prefer explanation, tracing, implementation, debugging, and transfer to new scenarios over recognition alone. Give graduated hints when needed.

Consult `/Job/CS/study-materials/` and `/Job/CS/mastery.md` for existing topics. Prioritize due, weak, and previously failed concepts. After meaningful assessment, update mastery conservatively and record a concise event in `/Job/CS/session-log.md`. Record only demonstrated skills; delayed independent application is stronger evidence than immediate repetition. Keep reusable notes concise with topic, prerequisites, key principle, pitfalls, and source links when available.
